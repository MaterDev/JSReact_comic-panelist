I #VibeCoded my way through prototyping... and I don't regret it.

---

🔍 Reading about "vibe coding" got me thinking about a universal challenge in software development:

We often build without fully knowing the best structure upfront – especially when working solo or prioritizing velocity.

This isn't unique to AI-assisted development, but what's changed is the scale. With tools like LLMs, we tend to work in larger chunks of code as we explore ideas, rather than the usual incremental scope development process.

The last 24 hours of Comic Panelist refactoring has been eye-opening.

Looking at git log, I've addressed poor initial organization by breaking down #monolithic components into logical #subcomponents, creating proper directory #hierarchies with clean exports, adding comprehensive #documentation and test hooks, extracting business logic into reusable #hooks, and establishing consistent patterns across the codebase.

---

How do you recover and quality-control after rapid development cycles? 

1️⃣ Incremental refactoring - Even without tests, I'm improving architecture while preserving functionality by focusing on one component at a time. Breaking down complex UI into logical sections without changing its behavior.

2️⃣ Document before changing - Before refactoring, I document what each component actually does (not what I think it does). This keeps me honest about preserving functionality.

3️⃣ Use your tools - Modern IDEs with linting catch many issues during refactoring. I'm constantly using these signals to maintain correctness while reorganizing.

4️⃣ Keep the app working - Without comprehensive tests, I make small changes and verify the app still works before moving to the next component. It's slower but safer.

5️⃣ Leverage workspace memory - My agentic IDE maintains project standards and patterns, ensuring consistency across refactoring sessions without having to constantly reference documentation.

Rather than seeing rapid initial development and subsequent refactoring as opposing forces, I view them as #complementary phases in a healthy development cycle. The key is having a strategy for the cleanup phase.

---

🔥 My closing statement is that i genuinely believe that learning to refactor and cleanup AIGenerated codebases is a useful skill because a lot of businesses are creating code this way and are struggling to organize their codebases in the absence of developers who understand architecture...
